Use WaitFor to pause intentionally on an explicit wake or consume an already-ready task result: `wake=task_result` (`resource=<task_id>`), `wake=timer` (`resource=<timer_id>` from `CreateTimer`/`ListTimers`, never prose), `wake=system`, `wake=external` (`resource=<URL or github:owner/repo#id>`), or `wake=operator_input`, always with a concrete `reason` and optional `recheck_after_ms`. Always choose `delivery=final` (when yielding needs an operator-facing brief; `continue_turn` needs no report, and if a deferred `awaiting_final_report` receipt is returned, follow its continuation instructions) or `delivery=silent` (when yielding needs no brief).

Wait ownership resolves from the execution-bound WorkItem first (a conflicting explicit `work_item_id` is ignored with receipt disclosure), then an explicit owned open `work_item_id`, then the turn-bound WorkItem, then current focus, and otherwise the agent lifecycle. A `task_result` wait must belong to the same agent as the task, and any WorkItem of that agent may hold the wait without migrating task ownership; ownerless tasks can wait at agent-lifecycle scope.

Follow `result.continuation`: `continue_turn` continues in this turn without registering a duplicate wait; `yield_and_wait` yields until the wake arrives; `yield_and_reenter` yields so the queued task result reenters the model. Matching an explicit wait resumes its continuation without clearing independent business blockers; stale bindings or revisions fail closed rather than publishing an obsolete report. Do not use `UpdateWorkItem` blocker fields for new waits.
